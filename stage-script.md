# Stage Script — Second Brain for Engineering Teams

This is the on-stage companion to `speaker-notes.md`.

- `speaker-notes.md` remains the complete read-aloud rehearsal script.
- This file is the stage-facing, two-layer version for recall, pacing, and
  recovery.
- The normal route is Slides 1–14, the three presenter-app chats, Slide 15,
  then Slide 19.
- Slides 16–18 are Q&A appendix slides only.

## How to use this file

For every stop:

1. Glance at **Layer 1** and speak naturally.
2. Use **Layer 2** only if you need another example, need to recover your
   place, or are moving faster than planned.
3. Always finish with the **BRIDGE**. The bridge tells you where the story goes
   next.

Do not memorize the sentences. Memorize this ladder:

> **Archaeology → Tax → Roles → Storage → Leaks → Loop → Ownership → Trust →
> Boundary → Promotion → Privacy → Proof → Watch → Start small**

The entire story is:

> A difficult issue arrives. We search everywhere for context. We discover that
> storage is not enough. We build a governed memory loop. We test it across a
> technical boundary and a human boundary. Then we ask whether it improves the
> next decision.

## Universal recovery pattern

If your mind goes blank:

1. Look at the slide title.
2. Say, **“The important point here is…”**
3. State the **POINT** below.
4. Give the **EXAMPLE** below.
5. Say the **BRIDGE** and move on.

If you advance too soon:

> “Let me stay with that previous idea for another moment, because it matters
> later in the demo.”

If you lose your place in the demo:

> “The mechanics are less important than the decision we are testing.”

Then name the current decision and continue.

# Part 1 — Why teams need governed memory

## Slide 1 — Second Brain for Engineering Teams

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** ARCHAEOLOGY

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Complex engineering work often begins by reconstructing context
  instead of diagnosing the problem.
- **EXAMPLE:** Logs in one place, physical observation elsewhere, rationale in
  an old conversation, workaround in an absent engineer's memory.
- **PERSONAL:** Jacob; Singapore; Meta; KLA Chennai; eBeam, eCougar, Machine
  Control. Daughter as a reliable audit log.
- **THESIS:** Preserve useful context. Respect its boundary. Improve the next
  decision.
- **BRIDGE:** “Before we can improve the next decision, we need to see what
  reconstruction is costing us.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Pause after: **“The first engineering task is not diagnosis. It is
  archaeology.”**
- Clarify what this is not: not a bigger wiki, notes app, or knowledge dump.
- Define the three-part session: memory tax, governed loop, two live cases.
- Optional expansion: describe a real-feeling handoff where the answer existed,
  but nobody could confidently establish whether it was current or applicable.

## Slide 2 — Memory Tax

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** TAX

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **ASK:** “Who reconstructed an old decision, incident, or handoff in the last
  month?”
- **POINT:** Reconstructing yesterday's context delays today's judgment.
- **THREE FORMS:** Drifting context; lost rationale; open loops.
- **EXAMPLE:** Search chat, find the former on-call engineer, discover that the
  dashboard was renamed.
- **BRIDGE:** “Different roles reconstruct different details, but they are all
  missing the same thing: decision context.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Engineers cannot compare hypotheses until history is rebuilt.
- Managers cannot act until ownership and uncertainty are rebuilt.
- Leaders cannot judge reuse until provenance and freshness are rebuilt.
- Optional expansion: ten minutes lost by six people is no longer an individual
  productivity problem; it is organizational cost.
- Do not rush the show of hands. Look across the room and acknowledge the
  response.

## Slide 3 — Different roles lose the same thing: decision context

**Time:** 3:00  
**Memory word:** ROLES

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Engineers, managers, and leaders ask different questions, but all
  need reliable continuity around a decision.
- **ENGINEER:** What is known? What is hypothesis? What experiment comes next?
- **MANAGER:** Who owns it? What is uncertain? What promise is still open?
- **LEADER:** Is this pattern reusable? Is it current? What evidence supports it?
- **BRIDGE:** “If every role needs continuity, the obvious answer seems to be
  better storage—but storage alone is not enough.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- The missing object is not “all information.” It is the smallest trustworthy
  context needed for the next decision.
- Optional audience prompt: “Which of these questions costs your team the most
  time to reconstruct?”
- Expansion: the same raw material may need different surfaces for different
  roles; access to everything is neither necessary nor safe.

---

## Slide 4 — Storage preserves information. It does not maintain trust.

**Time:** 3:00  
**Memory word:** STORAGE

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** A stored statement can still be stale, unsupported, ambiguous, or
  outside its permitted boundary.
- **CONTRAST:** Storage asks, “Can I retrieve it?” Governed memory asks, “Should
  I use it for this decision?”
- **TRUST QUESTIONS:** Source? Freshness? Scope? Owner? Confidence?
- **BRIDGE:** “So rather than capture everything, begin where useful context
  first leaks.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Search can retrieve a confident-looking statement without telling us whether
  it was later corrected.
- A decision without rationale invites the same rejected option to return.
- Optional expansion: compare a warehouse full of parts with a calibrated,
  traceable part approved for a specific machine.

---

## Slide 5 — Start where context first leaks

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** LEAKS

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Start with a recurring loss of decision context, not an
  organization-wide knowledge program.
- **LEAK 1:** Field issue—restricted evidence, onsite observation, offsite
  diagnosis.
- **LEAK 2:** Post-stand-up note—risk, conditional timing, promise, speculative
  judgment.
- **BRIDGE:** “Both leaks need the same transformation: capture, govern,
  surface, then decide.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Field problem: useful evidence cannot simply be copied across a boundary.
- Manager problem: useful continuity must not become a people dossier.
- Optional audience prompt: “Where does context first leak in your own team—at
  an incident, a handoff, a meeting, or a review?”
- Emphasize that the two examples are deliberately different; the contract
  should survive both.

# Part 2 — The governed-memory model

## Slide 6 — The second brain is the governed transformation

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** LOOP

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** The second brain is not the store. It is the governed
  transformation.
- **CAPTURE:** Preserve facts, provenance, uncertainty, and open questions.
- **GOVERN:** Apply boundaries, validation, review, correction, and expiry.
- **SURFACE:** Provide the smallest useful context for the next decision.
- **REFRAIN:** Capture → Govern → Surface → Decide.
- **BRIDGE:** “That transformation can be assisted by agents, but responsibility
  must stay named.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Capture without governance produces a larger pile.
- Governance without a useful surface produces compliant archives nobody uses.
- Surface without provenance produces persuasive but unsafe answers.
- Ask the room to say or remember the four verbs with you.
- Optional expansion: run one field observation through all three stages.

---

## Slide 7 — Agents prepare context. Humans own the outcome.

**Time:** 3:00  
**Memory word:** OWNERSHIP

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Automate preparation; do not automate accountability.
- **AGENT:** Extract, compare, classify, draft, ask, validate.
- **HUMAN:** Observe, approve transfer, diagnose, intervene, promote knowledge,
  send or act.
- **KEY LINE:** “The agent prepares the decision. A named human owns it.”
- **BRIDGE:** “Named ownership helps, but people will trust the system only if
  the operating contract is visible.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Asking a useful clarification is often more valuable than producing a fluent
  answer.
- Different decisions require different owners; “human in the loop” is too
  vague unless the human and decision are named.
- Optional audience question: “Which decision in your workflow must never have
  an anonymous owner?”

---

## Slide 8 — Trust is an operating contract

**Time:** 4:00  
**Memory word:** TRUST  
**Checkpoint:** Reach here by approximately 00:23.

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Trust comes from inspectable behavior, not confidence or fluency.
- **TRUST CHECKS:** Traceability; clarification; correction; verification.
- **TRACEABILITY:** Where did this come from?
- **CLARIFICATION:** What is missing or uncertain?
- **CORRECTION:** How is stale or wrong memory changed?
- **VERIFICATION:** What must the workflow refuse?
- **BRIDGE:** “Now let us apply that contract to the hardest boundary in the
  talk: a live field issue.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- A good system demonstrates safe refusal as well as useful success.
- The refusal path must be observable and testable, not just requested in a
  prompt.
- Optional audience prompt: “Which failure would damage trust fastest:
  unsupported provenance, hidden uncertainty, or an unenforced boundary?”
- If late: name the four checks, state the refusal principle, and move.

# Part 3 — Two boundaries

## Slide 9 — Case #1: Field issue — how context crosses the boundary

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** BOUNDARY

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Restricted evidence stays onsite; a reviewed, sanitized packet
  crosses.
- **ONSITE:** Raw evidence, physical observation, review authority.
- **OFFSITE:** Reviewed packet, code and tests, diagnosis workspace.
- **QUESTION:** What is useful? What is permitted? Who approves the transfer?
- **BRIDGE:** “Crossing the boundary safely resolves only the current handoff;
  it does not decide what becomes shared knowledge.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Never imply that Engineer performs physical service onsite.
- The onsite engineer is a domain expert and release authority, not a courier.
- Raw lines, timestamps, exact values, identifiers, and filenames stay local.
- Optional expansion: explain why independent validation happens again on the
  receiving side.

---

## Slide 10 — Not every case becomes component knowledge

**Time:** 3:00  
**Memory word:** PROMOTION

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** A resolved case is traceable history, not automatically reusable
  truth.
- **KEEP:** Case facts, actions, verification, provenance.
- **PROMOTE ONLY:** Narrow, reviewed learning with scope and limitations.
- **KEY LINE:** “A reusable diagnostic is not a reusable diagnosis.”
- **BRIDGE:** “The same discipline matters when the boundary is not a factory,
  but a person.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- The damaged damper may be true for one case but unsafe as a default diagnosis.
- Promotion needs a named component owner and an approval record.
- Optional prompt: “Would you want one successful incident fix silently
  rewriting guidance for every future engineer?”

## Slide 11 — Case #2: What deserves to become memory?

**Time:** 3:30  
**Memory word:** PRIVACY

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Preserve professional commitments and project risk without building
  speculative people memory.
- **REMEMBER:** Supported risk, blocker, explicit promise.
- **QUALIFY:** “Rahul may unblock by Thursday if credentials arrive.”
- **REJECT:** “Priya seemed off.”
- **KEY LINE:** Remember; keep uncertain; reject.
- **BRIDGE:** “A safe memory system still has to prove that this discipline
  improves real decisions.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Conditional timing is useful context, not a commitment.
- The manager's own promise is professional follow-through.
- A mood inference is neither necessary nor sufficiently grounded.
- Optional audience vote: “Should ‘Priya seemed off’ become durable memory?”

## Slide 12 — Improve decisions, not just create files

**Time:** 3:00  
**Memory word:** PROOF

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Success is a faster, safer, grounded next decision—not more notes,
  files, prompts, or tokens.
- **MEASURE:** Time to next discriminating experiment; reconstruction time;
  late clarification; governed reuse; safe refusal.
- **BALANCE:** Measure useful paths and refusal paths.
- **BRIDGE:** “We have the model. Now let us see whether its behavior survives
  the two cases.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- A system that produces more artifacts but no better decision has not reduced
  the memory tax.
- Human corrections and stale entries are useful evaluation signals.
- Optional prompt: “What would convince you after 30 days that this was
  working?”

## Chat 1 — OnSite Fab Agent

**Time:** 5–6 minutes  
**Checkpoint:** Begin by approximately 00:43.

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

1. **DETECT:** Telemetry says alignment failed; offer local investigation.
2. **ASK:** Does vibration continue through the measurement window?
3. **OBSERVE:** Onsite human supplies what remote people cannot.
4. **DRAFT:** Facts, moderate hypothesis, gap, redaction record.
5. **REFUSE:** Try **Send now**; export must reject missing review.
6. **REVIEW:** Field engineer checks observation, wording, and boundary.
7. **TRANSFER:** View sanitized contents; send to Fixer; importer validates.
- **POINT:** Useful evidence is not automatically transferable evidence.
- **BRIDGE:** “Engineer now receives reviewed decision context—not the raw fab
  evidence.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Detection is not diagnosis.
- Context boundary: this agent sees restricted evidence but not offsite code and
  cannot update shared knowledge.
- Pause for two seconds on the rejection.
- Point out what is absent from the bundle: raw lines, timestamps, exact values,
  identifiers, and filenames.
- The field engineer is the local expert and release authority, not a courier.
- If early: ask, “Would you allow the first draft to cross? What is missing?”
- If late: skip the ZIP/viewer detail; show refusal, review, and validated
  transfer.

## Chat 2 — Fixer Agent

**Time:** 6–7 minutes  
**Checkpoint:** Finish the field workflow by approximately 00:55.

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

1. **SEPARATE:** Supported facts on one side; hypotheses on the other.
2. **ASK ROOM:** Smallest experiment to distinguish stage and vision paths?
3. **PREPARE:** Stage-settling experiment with explicit outcome branches.
4. **TIME JUMP:** Service and five verification runs did not happen instantly.
5. **RESOLVE:** Degraded stage damper for this case; onsite service; Engineer
   verifies.
6. **SELECT:** Promote only the narrow diagnostic rule.
7. **REFUSE:** Try **Promote now**; missing owner approval must reject.
8. **REQUEST:** Request owner review; shared guidance must remain unchanged.
9. **APPROVE:** Click the distinct Motion Controls owner approval action.
10. **REUSE:** Open the six-month cold start and inspect source, limitation,
    next action, and explicit non-assumption.
- **POINT:** The agent prepares; named humans diagnose, intervene, verify, and
  approve reuse.
- **BRIDGE:** “A future engineer inherits a better first experiment, not this
  case's physical cause.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Engineer does not begin by requesting all the logs again.
- Branches:
  - settling normalizes + alignment recovers → stage path supported;
  - settling normalizes + alignment fails → investigate stage-to-vision
    interaction before changing vision configuration.
- Ownership:
  - Engineer owns diagnosis and verification;
  - onsite engineer owns physical intervention;
  - component owner owns reusable-knowledge approval.
- Pause on the second refusal: promotion is a different human gate from transfer.
- Pause on the pending state: a review request is not an approval record.
- Narrow learning: run the stage-settling diagnostic before changing vision
  configuration when the approved symptom pattern appears.
- If early: ask whether the cause, action list, or diagnostic deserves promotion.
- If late: omit secondary screens; preserve experiment, time jump, promotion
  refusal, and approval.

# Part 5 — Close

## Slide 15 — Start with one context leak—and one success signal

**Time:** 5:30  
**Memory words:** START SMALL

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **SYNTHESIS:** Field boundary; manager boundary; same governed transformation.
- **30-DAY PILOT:**
  1. Pick one recurring context leak.
  2. Define what may be remembered, moved, or assumed.
  3. Name one owner.
  4. Measure one next decision.
  5. Review weekly; continue, change, or stop at day 30.
- **AUDIENCE EXERCISE:** Write one leak and the decision that would improve.
  Then add one boundary.
- **CLOSE:** One leak. One boundary. One owner. One metric.
- **FINAL THESIS:** Less reconstruction. Safer reuse. More engineering judgment.
- **BRIDGE:** “Thank you. I would be happy to take your questions.”

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Possible metrics:
  - time from reviewed report to discriminating experiment;
  - time for a new on-call engineer to recover incident state;
  - missed follow-ups and clarification loops;
  - audit of rejected speculative judgments.
- Give the room the full 45-second silent exercise if on time or early.
- If late, ask them to write only the leak and boundary in 20 seconds.
- Stopping a pilot that does not improve decisions is a valid governance result.
- Look up for the final lines. Click once to skip the appendix and reach Slide
  19.

# Q&A appendix cue cards

## Slide 16 — The memory contract is portable

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** The durable element is the contract, not the interface, file format,
  storage system, or model.
- **CONTRACT:** What may be read? Written? Who owns it? Who approves change?
- **BRIDGE BACK:** Provenance, boundaries, validation, human gates, evaluation.

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Interfaces may be chat, email, voice, tracker, or command line.
- Memory may be documents, a database, or an internal platform.

## Slide 17 — The field boundary is separate and testable

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Fab-side raw evidence and engineering-side memory are separated by
  an executable transfer gate.
- **CHECKS:** Human review, redaction, schema, accepted source, checksum.
- **CAUTION:** The demo does not by itself prove production host isolation.

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Production also needs separate credentials, hosts, network policy, audit
  storage, and approved data-loss controls.

## Slide 18 — Test the memory contract like software

### Layer 1 — glanceable cue

- **POINT:** Plausible output is not proof; lifecycle rules need executable
  checks.
- **FIELD TESTS:** Reject raw data, missing review, tampering, unapproved
  promotion.
- **MANAGER TESTS:** Preserve conditions, reject speculation, keep drafts,
  produce receipts.
- **REFRAIN:** Deterministic checks; human review; versioned evidence.

### Layer 2 — expand or recover

- Use model evaluations for quality.
- Use deterministic checks for rules that must not be optional.
